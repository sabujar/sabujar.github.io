About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the University of Alabama at Birmingham, where I teach machines to reason about how systems change over time. My research spans multivariate time-series forecasting, multimodal learning, and transformer architectures: with applications from satellites watching algae blooms to clinical data watching transplant patients. My recent architecture, STAR, won the Best Student Paper Award at IEEE IRI 2026; before all this I taught computers to read Bengali handwriting. My work has picked up 1,380+ citations (most of them, I like to think, while I sleep).

I am on the academic job market and seeking tenure-track assistant professor positions beginning Fall 2027. If your department cares about time, temporal reasoning, or scientific ML, let's talk.

Research interests: time-series forecasting; multimodal learning; transformer architectures; representation learning; scientific machine learning; longitudinal and temporal modeling; large language and vision-language models; computer vision.

Research

  • STAR: Spectral and Trend Adaptive Regime-Guided Forecasting. A lightweight multivariate forecaster that routes signals by spectral, trend, and cross-variable structure for long horizons. Best Student Paper, IEEE IRI 2026.
  • Counterfactual utility routing & cross-variable dynamics. Learning which forecasts to trust, and recovering directed cross-variable dynamics via counterfactual interventions (RIVET). Two manuscripts under review.
  • Multimodal algal bloom severity modeling. Deep learning over satellite imagery, climate, and elevation data for environmental monitoring.
  • Chronofy: time-aware retrieval-augmented generation. Teaching RAG systems to notice when their “facts” have expired.
  • Longitudinal prediction of BK virus-associated nephropathy. Modeling kidney-transplant patient trajectories from heterogeneous longitudinal data, with physicians and biomedical researchers.
  • Medical image & vision modeling. DICOM preprocessing, region extraction, OCR-based label recognition, patch-level spatial analysis.

Things I Believe (about research)

  • Time is a feature, not a nuisance: most “noise” is structure we haven't modeled yet.
  • A model earns its complexity. Lightweight and honest beats heavy and mysterious.
  • The best research question is one a domain expert actually loses sleep over.
  • Teaching and research are the same activity at different speeds.
  • If you can't reproduce it, you don't yet understand it.

Advice I'd Give a New Grad Student

  • Read the classics and today's arXiv. The field rhymes.
  • Reproduce a paper before you critique it: you'll learn more from the appendix than the abstract.
  • Keep a research log. Your future self is a forgetful collaborator.
  • Write early, write badly, then rewrite. Clarity is a debugging process.
  • Protect your attention like a scarce compute budget.
“In learning, you will teach, and in teaching, you will learn.”
