About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the University of Alabama at Birmingham, working on machine learning for complex temporal and scientific systems. My research spans multivariate time-series forecasting, multimodal learning, and transformer architectures, with applications across remote sensing, healthcare, and large-scale AI. My recent work on STAR, an adaptive architecture for long-horizon forecasting, received the Best Student Paper Award at IEEE IRI 2026. I previously interned with the Bixby ML team at Samsung Research America, and my work has accumulated 1,380+ citations.

I am on the academic job market and seeking tenure-track assistant professor positions beginning Fall 2027.

Research interests: time-series forecasting; multimodal learning; transformer architectures; representation learning; scientific machine learning; longitudinal and temporal modeling; large language and vision-language models; computer vision.

Research

  • STAR: Spectral and Trend Adaptive Regime-Guided Forecasting. A lightweight multivariate forecasting architecture that adaptively routes signals by spectral, trend, and cross-variable characteristics for long-horizon prediction. Best Student Paper, IEEE IRI 2026.
  • Counterfactual utility routing and cross-variable dynamics. Learning which forecasts to trust, and recovering directed cross-variable dynamics through counterfactual interventions (RIVET). Two manuscripts under review.
  • Multimodal algal bloom severity modeling. Deep learning over satellite imagery, climate variables, and elevation data for environmental monitoring.
  • Chronofy: time-aware retrieval-augmented generation. A temporal-reasoning framework that reduces temporal hallucination from outdated retrieved content.
  • Longitudinal prediction of BK virus-associated nephropathy. Modeling kidney-transplant patient trajectories from heterogeneous longitudinal clinical data, with physicians and biomedical researchers.
  • Medical image and vision modeling. DICOM preprocessing, region extraction, OCR-based label recognition, and patch-level spatial analysis.

Teaching and Experience

My teaching rests on two convictions: that education is fundamentally about human development, and that a student's time is their most valuable resource. Inspired by Randy Pausch's The Last Lecture, I pair rigorous fundamentals with hands-on projects and mentor students toward independent inquiry: because, as I like to put it, in learning you will teach, and in teaching you will learn.

Courses taught: Artificial Intelligence, Machine Learning, Data Mining, Algorithms, Data Structures, Object-Oriented Programming, Database Systems, Computer Fundamentals.
