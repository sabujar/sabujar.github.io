About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at the University of Alabama at Birmingham, advised by Prof. Chengcui Zhang, with graduation expected in Spring 2027. I develop adaptive machine-learning methods for heterogeneous temporal and multimodal data. My research connects multivariate time-series forecasting with healthcare AI, including longitudinal clinical prediction, multimodal fusion, and medical image analysis.

I am on the academic job market, seeking tenure-track assistant professor positions beginning Fall 2027.

I bring university teaching experience across core computer science and AI courses, experience developing curricula, and a record of mentoring undergraduate research through peer-reviewed publication. I founded and led the DIU NLP & ML Research Lab (2017–2021) and previously interned with the Bixby ML Team at Samsung Research America.

Research interests: adaptive machine learning; multivariate time-series forecasting; signal-aware representations; contextual encodings; mixture-of-experts routing; directed cross-variable dependency learning; longitudinal and multimodal healthcare AI.

Research

Adaptive multivariate time-series forecasting

I developed STAR, a regime-guided architecture that adapts spectral and trend representations using signal-derived characteristics of heterogeneous multivariate time series. STAR achieved the best or second-best average MSE on four of five benchmarks across forecasting horizons and received the IEEE IRI 2026 Best Student Paper Award. My ongoing work explores learned contextual encodings, adaptive mixture-of-experts routing, counterfactual utility estimation, and intervention-based learning of directed cross-variable dynamics.

Longitudinal and multimodal healthcare AI

I develop models for BK virus-associated nephropathy using longitudinal kidney-transplant records. A BK PCR trajectory model achieved ROC-AUC 0.981 and PR-AUC 0.809. A multimodal transformer integrates laboratory trajectories, medications, patient characteristics, and clinical-language-model representations through multi-resolution temporal encoding, context-conditioned fusion, and temporal-neighborhood attention. This work also evaluates zero- and few-shot LLM labeling of pathology narratives.

In medical image analysis, I developed a weakly supervised multiple-instance learning pipeline for false-positive reduction and case-level classification among suspected breast-cancer cases, combining DICOM preprocessing, tumor-region extraction, OCR-based margin-label detection, and patch-level spatial analysis.

Temporal information and low-resource language technologies

I contributed to Chronofy, a temporal-logical decay architecture for estimating the validity of retrieved information through time-sensitive decay and logical consistency modeling. My work also includes attention-based neural models for English-to-Bengali machine translation and abstractive summarization.

Low-resource vision and applied machine learning

I contributed to Ekush, a Bangla handwritten-character dataset with more than 673,000 instances across 242 classes, and the EkushNet convolutional recognition model. Other collaborations include body-fat estimation and multimodal harmful-algal-bloom detection using satellite imagery, climate variables, and elevation data.
